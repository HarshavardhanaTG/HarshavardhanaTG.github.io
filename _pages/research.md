I develop fluid neuromuscular interfaces using EMG, enabling seamless human-computer interaction through hand gestures, handwriting, and speech. My current work includes a wristband-based interface for decoding handwriting and gestures, and an orofacial EMG-based interface for reconstructing speech from silent articulations for people who cannot produce audible speech, such as after laryngectomy or radiation-related damage to speech articulators.

At UC Davis, I lead efforts to collect and open-source large-scale surface EMG datasets, involving hundreds of participants performing gestures, handwriting, and speech articulation. All our data and code are available to the research community to promote transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative progress. Learn more through my [projects](/MyoVerse/).

My broader vision is to build AI systems that augment human capability rather than replace it: systems that act, in Sendhil Mullainathan's phrase, like "[bicycles for the mind](https://sendhil.org/things-im-involved-in/)." I want to create egocentric, multimodal interfaces that understand human intent from natural signals such as muscle activity, gaze, motion, and speech, enabling people to interact with technology as fluidly and expressively as they interact with each other.
